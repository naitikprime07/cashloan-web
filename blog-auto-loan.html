What is an auto loan?

In an auto loan the lender pays the dealer, and the vehicle is registered in your name with the lender’s charge noted on the registration certificate. This is called hypothecation. When you finish repaying, the lender gives you a no-objection certificate (NOC) and the charge is removed.

You pay a down payment from your own savings, commonly 10 to 20 percent of the price, and finance the rest. Always ask for the effective annual interest rate. A “flat” rate looks lower than it really is because it does not reduce as you repay.

Who can apply

  • Salaried people with a stable job and regular income.
  • Self-employed people who can show income tax returns for 1 to 2 years.
  • Age is usually 21 or more, and up to 60 or 65 by the time the loan ends.
  • A good credit score gives you a lower rate and a bigger loan.

Documents you may need

  • Identity proof and address proof.
  • Income proof: salary slips and bank statements, or income tax returns if you are self-employed.
  • The dealer’s quotation or proforma invoice for the vehicle.
  • A passport-size photograph and, sometimes, your driving licence.

How to apply

  1. Set your total vehicle budget

    Think about the on-road price, which includes insurance, road tax and registration, not only the showroom price.

  2. Plan your down payment

    A bigger down payment lowers your EMI and the interest you pay.

  3. Check your credit score

    This decides the rate you are offered.

  4. Compare offers

    Ask banks, NBFCs and the dealer for their rate, processing fee and prepayment terms.

  5. Choose the vehicle and get a quotation

    The lender needs a written quotation from the dealer.

  6. Apply, get sanction and sign

    Read every page of the agreement before you sign.

  7. Pay, insure and register

    Pay the down payment, buy insurance and make sure the lender’s charge is noted on the registration certificate.

Smart tips before you borrow

  • Compare interest rates on a reducing-balance basis so that every offer is measured the same way.
  • A shorter tenure means a higher EMI but much less interest.
  • Keep a comprehensive insurance policy running. Lenders usually require it.
  • Ask about foreclosure charges before you sign, in case you want to close the loan early.
  • Check if optional extras such as extended warranty are really optional.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Choosing a vehicle only because the EMI looks small.
  • Taking a very long tenure. The vehicle loses value faster than the loan reduces.
  • Forgetting to collect the NOC and get the charge removed after the last EMI.
  • Accepting add-ons that were bundled into the loan without asking.

Common questions

Can I get an auto loan for a used vehicle?

Yes. Many lenders finance used vehicles, but the loan amount is usually lower, the tenure shorter and the rate higher than for a new one.

Should I take dealer finance or a bank loan?

Compare both. Dealer finance is convenient, but check the rate, the fees and any extras added to the price.

What happens if I miss EMIs?

The lender can charge penalties and report the delay to credit bureaus. If payments stay unpaid, the lender can take the vehicle back as the agreement allows.
