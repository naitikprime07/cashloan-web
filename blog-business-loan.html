What is a business loan?

A business loan is money lent to a proprietor, partnership, LLP or company for business use. The lender checks how long the business has run, its turnover and profit, your bank statements, your GST returns and your personal credit score.

There is more than one kind of business loan. Choosing the right kind matters as much as choosing the right lender.

Working capital loan or overdraft

For day-to-day costs such as stock, salaries and rent. With an overdraft you pay interest only on what you use.

Term loan

For machinery, a shop or an expansion. You repay fixed EMIs over a set number of years.

Mudra loan

For micro and small businesses. Categories are Shishu (up to ₹50,000), Kishore (up to ₹5 lakh), Tarun (up to ₹10 lakh) and Tarun Plus (up to ₹20 lakh). Check the latest limits with your bank.

Who can apply

  • Sole proprietors, partnership firms, LLPs and companies.
  • Businesses that have run for 1 to 3 years. Startups have fewer options.
  • A steady turnover that shows in your bank statements and GST returns.
  • A good personal credit score of the owner.

Documents you may need

  • KYC of the owner or all partners and directors.
  • Business proof such as Udyam registration, GST registration or a shop and establishment licence.
  • Bank statements for the last 6 to 12 months.
  • Income tax returns and financial statements for the last 2 years.
  • GST returns, and property papers if the loan is secured.

How to apply

  1. Decide the purpose and the amount

    Write down what the money will be used for and how it will earn back the cost.

  2. Pick the right type of loan

    Use working capital for daily needs and a term loan for assets that last.

  3. Check your credit reports

    Look at both your business and your personal credit report and fix any errors.

  4. Organise your documents

    Keep bank statements, returns and registrations in order.

  5. Compare lenders and schemes

    Ask about government schemes such as Mudra and credit guarantee schemes that can allow loans without collateral for eligible MSMEs.

  6. Apply and get evaluated

    The lender may visit your shop or office and ask questions about your business.

  7. Use the funds as planned

    Use the money only for the stated purpose and keep proof of spending.

Smart tips before you borrow

  • Keep business and personal bank accounts separate.
  • File your GST and income tax returns on time. They are the main proof of your income.
  • Match the loan period to the purpose. Use a short loan for stock and a longer one for machinery.
  • Keep EMIs at a level your cash flow can carry, even in slow months.
  • Ask about an overdraft or credit line if your needs change from month to month.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Using a short-term loan to buy long-term assets.
  • Mixing personal and business money.
  • Not keeping a cash cushion for slow months.
  • Skipping the fine print on fees and personal guarantees.

Common questions

Can a new business get a loan?

It is harder, since most lenders like to see 1 to 3 years of records. Government schemes, smaller loans and lenders that accept a co-applicant can help a new business.

Do I need collateral?

Not always. Small loans can be unsecured, and some MSME loans are covered by a credit guarantee. Bigger loans usually need security.

What is a business overdraft?

It is a limit on your current account. You can draw money when needed and pay interest only on the amount you use.
