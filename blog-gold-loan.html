What is a gold loan?

The lender tests the purity of your gold, weighs it and values it at the day’s market rate. You can borrow up to a percentage of that value, called the loan-to-value ratio. The gold stays in the lender’s vault and comes back to you when the loan is paid in full.

Repayment options differ. You may pay monthly interest and the principal at the end, pay regular EMIs, or pay everything in one go at the end. If you do not repay, the lender can auction the gold after giving you notice, so plan your repayment before you borrow.

What counts in the value

Only the weight of pure gold counts. Stones, wax and making charges are not valued.

Purity

Lenders usually accept 18 to 22 carat jewellery. Higher purity means a higher value.

Who can apply

  • Adult Indian residents who own gold jewellery or coins.
  • Income proof is usually not needed for small loans.
  • The gold must meet the lender’s purity rules, usually 18 carat or more.
  • KYC is compulsory for every borrower.

Documents you may need

  • Identity proof such as Aadhaar, passport or voter ID.
  • Address proof.
  • A passport-size photograph.
  • PAN card for larger loan amounts.
  • Sometimes proof of ownership of the gold, such as purchase bills, for larger quantities.

How to apply

  1. Know the value of your gold

    Check the weight and purity, and look at today’s gold rate to estimate what you can borrow.

  2. Compare lenders

    Compare the interest rate, loan-to-value, processing fee, valuation charge and repayment options.

  3. Visit the branch with your gold and KYC

    The valuation is done in front of you.

  4. Understand the terms

    Ask for the interest schedule, the due date and the auction rules in writing.

  5. Sign and receive the money

    You receive the amount in your bank account after the paperwork is complete.

  6. Repay on time

    Pay the interest or EMIs as agreed and keep every receipt.

  7. Collect your gold on closing

    Check the gold against your receipt before you take it back.

Smart tips before you borrow

  • Ask for the weight, purity and value of your gold in writing.
  • Compare the total cost, including the valuation and processing fees.
  • Choose a repayment plan that fits your income. Farm loans can be timed to the harvest.
  • Prefer a bank or an RBI-registered NBFC with a secure vault and insurance.
  • Set a reminder for the due date. Ask whether you can close the loan early without a penalty.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Not reading the auction terms.
  • Borrowing the maximum, even though gold prices can fall.
  • Leaving your gold with an unregistered or informal lender.
  • Missing the renewal or closing date.

Common questions

Does a gold loan affect my credit score?

Some lenders report to credit bureaus. Timely repayment helps your record, and missed payments can hurt it.

What if the price of gold falls?

If the value of your gold falls so that the loan is above the allowed limit, the lender may ask you to pay part of the loan or add more gold.

Is my gold safe with the lender?

Regulated lenders store gold in secured vaults, usually with insurance. Ask about it and get a receipt that lists every item.
