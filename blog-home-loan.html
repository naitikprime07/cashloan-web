What is a home loan?

The property is the security. The lender keeps the original property documents until you repay the loan in full. Because the risk is lower and the tenure is long, the interest rate is usually among the lowest of all common loans.

You pay the rest of the price as a down payment, at least 10 to 25 percent of the property value under the RBI’s loan-to-value rules. Most home loans have a floating rate linked to an external benchmark such as the repo rate, so your EMI or tenure can change when the rate changes. On floating-rate home loans to individuals, lenders cannot charge a prepayment penalty.

Floating rate

Moves with the benchmark. It can fall when rates fall and rise when rates rise. It is the most common choice.

Fixed rate

Stays the same for an agreed period. It gives certainty, but it is often a little higher and can reset later.

Who can apply

  • Salaried or self-employed people with a steady income.
  • Age is usually 21 or more, and up to 65 by the time the loan ends.
  • A good credit score. 750 or more usually gets the best rates.
  • A property with a clear title and the approvals of the local authority.

Documents you may need

  • Identity proof and address proof.
  • Income proof: 3 to 6 months of salary slips, Form 16 and bank statements. Self-employed people give 2 to 3 years of returns.
  • Property papers such as the sale agreement, title deed, approved plan and society or builder NOC.
  • For an under-construction home, the builder’s RERA registration details.
  • Passport-size photographs and the processing fee.

How to apply

  1. Work out what you can afford

    Keep the EMI within 40 to 50 percent of your income, and add the down payment, stamp duty and registration to your budget.

  2. Check your credit score

    A better score can bring a lower rate for the next 20 years.

  3. Get an in-principle sanction

    This shows you how much the lender is ready to give before you choose a property.

  4. Choose and verify the property

    Check the title, the approvals and, for under-construction homes, the RERA registration.

  5. Compare lenders

    Compare the rate, processing fee, legal and valuation charges and the rules on rate changes.

  6. Apply and complete verification

    The lender does a legal and technical check of the property and issues a sanction letter.

  7. Sign and get the loan disbursed

    For an under-construction home the money is usually paid in stages as the work progresses.

Smart tips before you borrow

  • Compare the total cost, including the processing fee, valuation and legal charges, and not just the rate.
  • Part-pay whenever you can, for example with a bonus. Prepaying early cuts a lot of interest. There is no prepayment penalty on floating-rate loans to individuals.
  • Under the old tax regime you may get a deduction on interest of up to ₹2 lakh under Section 24(b) and on principal up to ₹1.5 lakh under Section 80C. Rules change, so check with a tax adviser.
  • A joint loan with a spouse or a family member can raise your eligibility.
  • Look at government schemes, such as the PMAY interest subsidy, if you are eligible.
  • Keep an emergency fund that covers at least 6 months of EMIs.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Borrowing the maximum amount you are offered.
  • Forgetting extra costs such as stamp duty, registration, interiors and maintenance.
  • Not checking the title and approvals of the property.
  • Ignoring how and when the interest rate can change.

Common questions

Should I choose a fixed or a floating rate?

Most borrowers choose floating because it follows the market and has no prepayment penalty. A fixed rate gives certainty for the agreed period. Compare the total cost of both.

What is a home loan balance transfer?

It means moving your loan to another lender with a lower rate. Check the fees and calculate the real savings before you move.

What happens to the property papers after I repay?

The lender returns the original documents and gives you a no-dues certificate. Make sure the lender’s charge on the property is removed.
