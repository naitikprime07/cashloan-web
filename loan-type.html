Loan types

Understand each loan type before you choose

Every loan is built for a different purpose, with its own amount, period and interest rate. Picking the right type helps you avoid paying for features you don't need.

Personal Loan

For any personal need such as medical costs, a wedding or travel. Usually unsecured, so no collateral is needed.

Typical period: 1 to 5 years

Auto Loan

To buy a new or used vehicle. The vehicle acts as security until the loan is fully repaid.

Typical period: 1 to 7 years

Student Loan

For tuition, hostel, books and other course costs. Repayment often starts only after the course ends.

Period: course duration plus repayment years

Business Loan

For working capital, stock, equipment or expansion. Lenders check your business income and how long it has been running.

Typical period: 1 to 5 years

Payday Loan

A small short-term loan that you repay from your next salary. It is quick to get, but the cost is high, so use it only in a real emergency.

Typical period: 1 to 3 months

Home Loan

To buy, build or renovate a home. The property is the security, with long periods and usually lower interest rates.

Typical period: up to 30 years

Gold Loan

Borrow against your gold jewellery or coins. It is quick to get, with little paperwork.

Typical period: 3 months to 3 years

Secured vs unsecured

Secured

Backed by an asset like a house or vehicle. Usually lower rates and longer periods.

Unsecured

No asset needed. Approval depends on income and credit score, and rates are usually higher.

Choosing wisely

  • Match the loan type to your purpose. A dedicated loan often has better terms than a general one.
  • Compare the total cost, not just the EMI. Look at interest, processing fee and other charges.
  • Check what happens if you repay early or miss a payment.

Common questions

Can I use a personal loan for anything?

Usually yes for genuine personal needs, though some lenders do not allow it for investments or speculation. Check the terms.

Which loan type has the lowest interest?

Secured loans such as home and auto loans are generally cheaper than unsecured ones, but the rate also depends on the lender and your profile.
